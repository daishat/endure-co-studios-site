Narrative Design Sample | Psychological Horror

An original project by Endure Co Studios

About the Project

The House Remembers is a psychological horror game I'm developing around the idea that a house can hold on to things people would rather forget.

I don't want the horror to come from something constantly jumping out at the player. I want it to build through little things that don't seem right at first. A room that looks different than it did a few minutes ago. Something familiar sitting where it shouldn't be. A detail that makes the player wonder whether they missed something or whether the house is changing around them.

The idea is to make exploration part of the storytelling. Players aren't just walking through rooms looking for clues. They're slowly figuring out what happened, what the house is hiding, and whether they can trust what they're seeing.

For this sample, I want to show how I'd build that experience through environmental details, character interactions, and choices that affect what the player discovers.

About the game

The House Remembers follows a woman who returns to her childhood home and begins noticing things she can’t explain. There are two beds in her old bedroom, even though she remembers sleeping alone. Faces have been scratched out of family photographs. Neighbors ask about a sister she does not remember having, but her father denies she ever existed.

The player explores the house, finds clues, and pieces together incomplete memories while her father interrupts the investigation. He genuinely loves his daughter, but he has convinced himself that keeping the truth from her is a way of protecting her. He is also protecting himself.

I want the player to feel that something is wrong with this family without knowing exactly what happened or how much of the protagonist’s memory can be trusted.

Quest: The Second Bed

Location: Childhood bedroom, upstairs hallway, forbidden room, dining room.

Starting Objective: Find out why there are two beds in the childhood bedroom.

Design Focus: Exploration, conditional dialogue, suspicion, timed opportunities, and remembered clues.

Scene 1- The bedroom

The bedroom is almost as she remembers it: the dresser, the curtains, the familiar arrangement of furniture, but a second bed stands across from hers. Examining it unlocks a question about who shared the room.

Protagonist: “Why are there two beds?”

Her dad appears in the doorway. Dad: “We used that room for storage. You really don’t remember?”

Protagonist: “Storage? It’s a bed.”

Dad: “We kept all kinds of stuff in there.”

Choice: Challenge his explanation, or let the subject drop. Challenging him makes him impatient; accepting it lets the player keep looking without an immediate argument.

Scene 2- The photograph and the neighbor

The player finds a family photograph with a face scratched out. The discovery is recorded, but her father

does not react unless the player chooses to show him.

Father: “Where’d you find that?”

Protagonist: “It was upstairs.”

Father: “Who have you been talking to?”

Mentioning that a neighbor remembers a sister makes him ask exactly which neighbor said it.

He discredits that person and begins checking on the protagonist more often, reducing uninterrupted search opportunities. Keeping the photograph hidden avoids that immediate escalation. Scene 3 — The forbidden room The protagonist approaches a room her father has told her to leave alone. Before she can enter, he catches her at the doorway. Father: “You need something? Because I know I told you to stay out of here.”

Protagonist: “I was just looking around.”

Father: “Then look somewhere else.”

He stays in the doorway until she backs away.

Quest update: Find another opportunity to enter the forbidden room. Nothing inside has been discovered yet.

Scene 4 — Dinner

Her father calls her downstairs and serves her favorite meal. He remembers what she liked as a child and asks about her day. If she presses him about her sister, his warmth fades.

Protagonist: “Why won’t you tell me about my sister?”

Father: “You know me. You really think I’d hurt you?”

The player may confront him, thank him for dinner, or stay quiet. He can be affectionate and controlling in the same conversation. His concern for her is real, but he expects their closeness to end the questioning.

After dinner, he asks her to bring down the laundry from the upstairs hamper. He turns on the kitchen sink to wash the dishes. Scene 5 — A second chance

Upstairs, the player hears running water. The forbidden room is close by. The ordinary sound of dishes being washed becomes the signal that she has a short window to investigate.

Option A — Search the room. She enters, finds a small yellow hair clip with one broken petal, and examines it. The player can pocket it or put it back. Either way, the discovery is recorded and a new question becomes available later.

Option B — Bring down the laundry first. She completes the chore. Her father remains unsuspecting, but this search opportunity closes. The player must find another opening.

Option C — Ask him about the room again. The player gets additional dialogue, but he watches her more closely afterward, making future searches harder.

Scene 6 — The yellow hair clip The clip is small and worn, with one broken petal. Holding it triggers a brief, incomplete memory: someone sits behind the protagonist, struggling to fasten the clip in her hair. She remembers laughing.

She cannot remember the person’s face.

The memory does not explain who the person was or what happened. It gives the player something personal to hold against the father’s insistence that there was never another child.

Take the clip: It enters the inventory. If she hides it before her father arrives, he does not

automatically know she took anything. He can learn only if he sees it or later notices it missing.

Leave the clip: The clue remains in the room. The discovery and memory are still recorded, but the physical object is not in the inventory.

Scene 7 — When the water stops

While the player is searching, the sink stops running. This is the first warning. If she lingers, footsteps approach the stairs, giving one last chance to leave.

If she leaves in time: She avoids being caught. The clue remains recorded whether or not she took the clip. The father’s knowledge does not change unless she has already confronted him or left evidence of her search.

If he catches her: He stands at the doorway. He is quieter than before.

Father: “Put it back.”

Protagonist: “You told me there was nothing in here.”

Father: “I asked you to bring down the laundry.”

He waits until she leaves, then locks the room. He stays nearby and checks on her more often afterward.

The investigation continues, but the player needs another way inside. Any clue already examined stays recorded.

If the clip is visible, he may recognize that she handled it. If she pocketed it before he arrived, he knows she entered the room but does not know she took the clip unless he later sees it or notices it missing.

Quest logic and consequences

Examine second bed Unlock the question about who shared the room.

Find scratched photograph Record discovery; father reacts only if shown.

Mention neighbor and sister Father asks who spoke to her; future interruptions increase.

Caught at forbidden-room doorway Access denied; new objective to find another opening.

Dinner conversation Confrontation raises tension; playing along preserves a calmer interaction.

Bring laundry down first Preserves low suspicion; misses this search window.

Search while dishes run Find a yellow clip and trigger partial memory.

Pocket clip Add to inventory; father has no automatic knowledge of theft.

Leave clip Discovery persists; physical evidence stays in the room.

Water stops Warning; footsteps follow if player delays

Caught after warning Room locked; father patrols more often; recorded clues persist.

Why I designed it this way

The father is not a stranger the player can simply dismiss. He remembers her favorite dinner, worries about her getting home safely, and genuinely loves her. That makes it harder for her to accept that he might be lying. The player can challenge him, but doing so changes how closely he watches her. Playing along can make the investigation easier, even when it feels wrong.

I wanted a normal family routine to create the next gameplay opportunity. Washing dishes gives the protagonist a chance to search, and the water stopping tells the player that chance is ending. The clip adds an emotional clue without solving the mystery. Even getting caught does not erase what the player learned.

Portfolio note: This is a proposed narrative-design sequence for an original game in development. The interactions and conditions described here have not been implemented or playtested.
